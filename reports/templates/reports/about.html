About NDMS

Understanding the system behind disaster information.

NDMS is a web platform for disaster reporting and public awareness in Nepal. It brings incident reports, location information, disaster records and guidance into one structured system, and every report is reviewed before it becomes a disaster record.

What NDMS does

NDMS moves information through three stages, from the citizen who reports an incident to the public who relies on the result.

  1. 01

    Report

    Citizens submit structured incident information: what happened, how severe it is, where it happened and any photos.

  2. 02

    Review

    NDMS administrators check, organise and manage each report before it becomes a disaster record.

  3. 03

    Inform

    The result reaches people as disaster records, response updates, alerts and preparedness guidance.

Turning scattered incident information into structured, useful information.

Incident details can arrive in different forms and from different places, which makes them hard to compare and act on. NDMS gives citizens one consistent way to report, and gives administrators one place to review what comes in.

Structured information

Every report carries the same core details: disaster type, reported severity, description, when the incident began, location and photos. Reviewers compare like with like.

Location awareness

Each report is pinned on a map and given an address, so where an incident happened is part of the record rather than something to be guessed.

Transparent follow-up

A report does not disappear after submission. Citizens can follow its status, and disaster records carry response updates as the situation changes.

How NDMS works

Five steps take an incident from a citizen's report to information the public can follow. The label above each step shows who carries it out.

  1. 1

    Step 1, carried out by Citizen

    Submit

    A citizen files a report with the disaster type, severity, description and photos. It starts as Pending.

  2. 2

    Step 2, carried out by Citizen

    Locate

    Each report is pinned on a map and given an address, so reviewers know exactly where it happened.

  3. 3

    Step 3, carried out by Administrator

    Review

    An administrator moves the report to Under Review, then marks it Verified or Rejected.

  4. 4

    Step 4, carried out by Administrator

    Update

    A verified report can become a disaster record. Response updates are logged against the emergency agency involved.

  5. 5

    Step 5, visible to Everyone

    Inform

    Records, updates and alerts are published for citizens to follow, and report owners are notified as their report's status changes.

What the platform brings together

Reports, records and guidance sit in one system, so citizens and administrators work from the same information.

  • Incident reporting

    Structured submission of the disaster type, severity, description, start time and photos.

  • Location information

    A map pin and address on every incident, so readers can see where it happened.

  • Disaster records

    Organised records of reported events, with a type, severity, status and photos.

  • Updates

    Administrators add response updates and change a record's status as the situation develops.

  • Guidelines

    Preparedness and awareness guidance for common disaster risks.

  • Emergency agencies

    A public directory of emergency agencies with their contact numbers and locations.

Who NDMS is for

Two groups use NDMS directly: citizens and NDMS administrators. Their accounts and tools are kept separate.

Citizens

Anyone can read disaster information and guidance. Registered citizens can also report incidents.

  • Report incidents with type, severity, description, location and photos
  • Follow the status of their own reports
  • Browse disaster records and response updates
  • Read preparedness guidance
  • Find emergency agency contacts
  • Receive notifications and alerts

NDMS administrators

Administrators work from a separate dashboard that citizens cannot open.

  • Review submitted reports and update their status
  • Create disaster records from verified reports
  • Manage disaster types and emergency agencies
  • Record response updates and publish notifications
  • Review citizen feedback and support requests
  • Monitor system activity from the dashboard

Better disaster information starts with timely reporting.

Submit a structured report with the disaster type, severity, location and photos. NDMS administrators review every report. NDMS is not an emergency dispatch service, so contact the designated emergency authorities directly when lives are at immediate risk.
